Thirdline User Guide

Thirdline makes animated lower thirds (the name-and-title graphics at the bottom of a video) and exports them with a transparent background, ready to drop on top of your footage.

Your first lower third (2 minutes)

  1. Pick a style. On the start screen, or in step 1 of Easy mode, click any style. The filters at the top (News, Podcast, Sport…) narrow the list. Thumbnails show your own text.
  2. Type your text. Fill in the name and title in step 2, or double-click the text in the preview.
  3. Choose how it moves. Step 3 sets the in and out animation, the speed and how long it stays on screen. Drag the In / Hold / Out bar to change the timing.
  4. Place it. Drag it in the preview. It snaps to the title-safe and action-safe guides; hold Alt to move without snapping.
  5. Export. Click Export…, pick the app you edit in under Made for, and choose where to save. You get a video with a transparent background.

Press Space to play the animation. Press ` to hide the guides and see it clean.

Easy mode

Easy mode is for getting a good-looking graphic fast, without any animation knowledge. Everything happens in three steps on the left and right panels.

  • Fonts and colours: pick a font pairing and the three main colours. Any font installed on your computer is available, or upload a .ttf, .otf, .woff or .woff2 file.
  • Per-part animation: the backdrop, the name and the title can each have their own in and out animation.
  • While on screen: a gentle pulse, float, drift or breathe keeps long holds from looking frozen.
  • Preview on footage: choose Preview on and pick a video or image (or drop one on the preview) to see the graphic over real footage. Footage is never exported.

Exporting

FormatTransparencyBest for
MOV · ProRes 4444YesPremiere Pro, Final Cut Pro, DaVinci Resolve, After Effects
WebM · VP9YesOBS, vMix, web players. Small files.
PNG sequenceYesAnything that reads image sequences. One PNG per frame.
MP4 · H.264NoSocial video. Rendered over a colour you choose (use green to key it later).
  • Made for sets the right format for your editor and tells you how to bring it in.
  • Frame rate: match your timeline. 23.976, 29.97 and 59.94 are written as exact broadcast rates (24000/1001 and so on), so editors don't flag a mismatch.
  • Also make 9:16 and 1:1: tick these to get vertical and square versions in the same export, re-placed for that frame.
  • Use GPU and hardware encoding: on a Mac this draws frames on the graphics card and uses Apple's hardware encoder for ProRes and H.264. If the hardware path fails, Thirdline quietly falls back to the regular encoder.
  • Set a default export folder in Settings (⌘,). Thirdline sends a notification when a long export finishes while you're in another app.

Using it in your editor

Premiere Pro

Import the .mov and drag it onto a video track above your footage (V2). Transparency works automatically. For a template editors can retype, see .mogrt.

DaVinci Resolve

Import the .mov into the Media Pool and place it on a track above your footage. If the background shows black, right-click the clip, choose Clip Attributes and set Alpha mode to Straight.

Final Cut Pro

Import the .mov and connect it above your footage in the timeline.

OBS and vMix

Export WebM and add it as a Media Source, or use Live output to trigger lower thirds during a stream.

Projects, styles and brands

  • Projects (.l3rd) save everything, including uploaded fonts and images, so a project opens the same on another computer. Fonts installed on your system can't be packed into the file; if one is missing when you open a project, Thirdline tells you which.
  • Autosave: your current design is saved as you work. If Thirdline closes unexpectedly it comes back where you left off.
  • My styles: File › Save design as a style keeps a look you can reuse. Saved styles show under My styles in the gallery.
  • Brand kits: Brand ▾ saves your colours, fonts and logo. While a brand is on, every style in the gallery uses it. Use Export brand kit to share it with a teammate or move it to a new Mac, and Import brand kits on the other side.

Advanced mode

Switch with the Easy / Advanced buttons at the top. Advanced mode adds layers, keyframes, a graph editor and the full timeline, and works much like After Effects.

  • Left panel: Layers, Animation presets, Styles and Assets tabs.
  • Tool rail in the preview: Select (V), Pen (P), Mask pen (⇧P), shapes, text, image, controller, Hand (H).
  • Inspector on the right has Style, Motion and Position tabs for the selected layer. Click a colour's ▦ button for swatches and recent colours.
  • Selection bar above the preview: align buttons and Parent.

Timeline and keyframes

  • Click the ◇ next to a property to add a keyframe at the playhead. Keyframes show on the timeline under their layer.
  • Drag keyframes to retime them. Shift-click or drag a box to select several. J / K jump between keyframes.
  • Right-click a keyframe to change its easing. Double-click it (or press Graph…) for the graph editor.
  • The ◆ pairs on each layer bar are its preset In and Out animations. Click one to change it, drag to retime, or Convert to keyframes to edit it key by key.
  • Timeline switches: MB turns motion blur on or off per layer, Matte uses the layer above as a track matte, and the colour chip is the layer's label.
  • Alt + scroll zooms the timeline, Shift + scroll pans, double-click the ruler to fit.

Layers, masks and the pen

  • + Add in the Layers tab adds text, a logo or image, accent bars, lines, shapes, a controller (an invisible null to parent to) or a group.
  • Parenting: set a layer's Parent and it follows that layer's position, rotation and scale.
  • Groups: ⌘G groups the selected layers; ⇧⌘G ungroups.
  • Pen: click to add points, drag for curves, click the first point (or press Enter) to finish. Trim paths animate a stroke drawing on.
  • Masks: rectangle, ellipse or pen masks per layer with feather and invert, or the quick wipe, rise and iris presets.
  • Text animators: After Effects-style range selectors that animate letters or words.

Batch export from a spreadsheet

  1. Make a spreadsheet with a column for names and one for titles (one row per person). Save it as CSV (in Google Sheets: File › Download › CSV).
  2. In Thirdline choose File › Batch export from a spreadsheet, pick the CSV and match the columns to your text layers.
  3. Pick a format and a folder. Thirdline renders one file per row, named after the row.

Live output for OBS

  1. Click Go live (or File › Live output for OBS) and turn it on.
  2. In OBS add a Browser source with the URL shown (http://127.0.0.1:7461/live) and your canvas size.
  3. Use the In and Out buttons, or a Stream Deck / Bitfocus Companion sending HTTP GET requests to /cue/in, /cue/out or /cue/toggle.

Changes you make in Thirdline show up live. The output is only reachable from this computer.

.mogrt for Premiere

A .mogrt is a template Premiere editors can retype in the Essential Graphics panel. Only After Effects can create one, so File › Export as .mogrt opens After Effects (2018 or newer; 2024+ recommended), rebuilds your design there and saves the .aep and .mogrt.

  • Save your work in After Effects first: Thirdline starts a new project.
  • Text, colours and logos become Essential Graphics controls. The backdrop keeps wrapping the text when an editor retypes it.
  • Textures, animated gradients and pen shapes are not carried over. Shadows, glows and blur are close approximations.

Fonts

  • 12 fonts are built in (Inter, Montserrat, Poppins, Oswald, Anton, Bebas Neue, Playfair Display and more).
  • Every font installed on your Mac is listed under On this computer.
  • Uploaded fonts are remembered and travel inside project files. Installed system fonts don't: install them on the other computer too.

Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
The background is black in my editorExport ProRes 4444, WebM or PNG (MP4 has no transparency). In Resolve, set the clip's Alpha mode to Straight.
"Missing fonts" when opening a projectInstall the named fonts, or pick another font. A fallback font is used until then.
Export failed with a hardware encoderUntick Use GPU and hardware encoding and export again. Report it so we can fix it.
.mogrt export doesn't startIn the .mogrt dialog click change… and pick your After Effects. Close any After Effects dialogs that are open.
The preview is slowTurn off motion blur preview, or lower the preview zoom.
Something elseHelp › Report a problem copies the details we need (versions, no project content).
